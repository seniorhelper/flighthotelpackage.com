Short answer

A flight and hotel package is worth booking when the bundled total comes in below the sum of the cheapest separate flight and the cheapest separate hotel for the same dates — which happens more often than sceptics expect, because hotels release discounted rates to package sellers that they are contractually barred from advertising publicly. It is the wrong choice when you are using airline points, flying a budget carrier that packages exclude, need flexible cancellation on one half of the trip, or are travelling somewhere with cheap independent accommodation. The honest premise

Every other package site is a funnel. This one shows you the arithmetic.

There is a specific reason bundles can undercut booking separately, and it is not generosity. Hotels supply discounted room rates to large travel sellers under contracts that forbid publishing those rates as a visible standalone price. The hotel gets to fill rooms it would otherwise leave empty without undermining the published rate that anchors what every other guest pays. The seller gets inventory nobody else can advertise.

The rate only becomes visible to you once it is buried inside a combined total. That is the entire mechanism, and it means the saving is real rather than a marketing illusion.

It also means the saving is conditional. It exists when the seller is holding discounted stock at the hotel you want, on the dates you want, paired with a flight you would actually have taken. Change any of those and the advantage evaporates — sometimes into an outright loss, because you have also given up flexibility, loyalty credit and the ability to use points.

So the useful question is never "are packages cheaper". It is "is this package cheaper than these two bookings, for me, on these dates".

That question has an arithmetic answer and takes about four minutes to work out. Almost nobody does it, because every site in this category is built to move you toward the booking button rather than toward the answer.

Why are flight and hotel packages cheaper than booking separately?

Because hotels release discounted wholesale rates to package sellers under contracts that prohibit displaying those rates publicly as a standalone price. Bundling the room with a flight hides the individual rate inside a combined total, which lets the hotel fill unsold inventory without undercutting the published rate other guests pay. The discount is real, but it only applies where the seller actually holds discounted stock at your hotel on your dates.

How do I know if a vacation package is actually a good deal?

Price the three things separately and add them up. Take the package total including taxes, then find the cheapest flight for the same dates and airports and the cheapest rate for the same hotel and room type, and add any baggage fee the package does not include. If the package total is lower, it is a genuine saving. If it is within a couple of percent either way, book separately, because the flexibility, the loyalty credit and the ability to change one half of the trip are worth more than a rounding error.

Five and five

When a package wins, and when it quietly loses.

These are not close calls. If your trip matches something in the second column, you can stop reading and book separately.

Book the package

1

Resort destinations where the hotel is most of the cost

Caribbean, Mexico, Hawaii, Florida. When accommodation is the larger half of the trip, a wholesale rate discount has more to work with. This is the single most reliable case for bundling.

2

Longer stays, seven nights and up

The discount compounds per night while the flight cost stays fixed. A week beats a weekend by a wide margin for exactly this reason.

3

Peak season at a full hotel

Counter-intuitive but real. When published rates spike, the contracted wholesale rate the seller holds was often set earlier and lower, so the gap between the package and the public price widens.

4

You want one transaction and one phone number

Worth something, particularly with children or a group. When a flight is cancelled, a package seller has an obligation covering both halves of your trip rather than pointing at the other supplier.

5

You have no points and no status to protect

If you are not chasing elite nights or holding miles worth redeeming, the biggest arguments against packages simply do not apply to you.

Book separately

1

You are redeeming miles or points

Packages are cash-only. A flight paid with miles or a hotel night on points beats almost any cash discount, and packages remove the option entirely.

2

The cheapest flight is on a carrier the package does not sell

Budget carriers are frequently absent from package inventory. If the low fare on your route is on an airline the seller does not carry, the package starts from a worse flight price and rarely recovers.

3

You need one half of the trip to stay flexible

Package cancellation terms are typically governed by the stricter of the two components. If there is a real chance of change, pay a little more for two bookings you can alter independently.

4

You are chasing hotel status or loyalty nights

Most chains exclude third-party wholesale and packaged rates from earning points and from counting toward elite tiers. If you are close to a tier, that exclusion frequently costs more than the discount saves.

5

Accommodation is cheap and easy to book locally

Much of Southeast Asia, Central America and southern Europe. Where good rooms are inexpensive and bookable direct, the wholesale discount is a small saving on an already small number, and you lose choice.

What to actually do with three conflicting answers

Take the overlap. Every source agrees that booking eleven months out is not the cheapest option, because airlines set opening prices before competition establishes the market rate. Every source agrees that booking inside two weeks is expensive. The disagreement is confined to the middle.

For domestic travel, the ranges overlap at roughly four to eight weeks ahead. For international, at roughly three to five months. Book inside those and you are within the recommended window of every major dataset simultaneously, which is the most defensible position available.

Then stop looking. The most expensive habit in travel is not booking at the wrong time; it is refusing to book while waiting for a slightly better price and watching the fare you liked disappear. Decide your acceptable number before you search, and book when you see it.

Why they disagree at all

Because they are measuring different things. Google Flights sees enormous volumes of search queries across every carrier. Expedia sees its own bookings, which skew toward package and leisure travellers. Going watches a curated set of routes and looks for outlier fares rather than averages.

None of them is wrong. They are describing different slices of the same market, and a site that presents one as the definitive answer is either unaware of the others or hoping you are.

Packages add a further wrinkle: the hotel half of a bundle follows hotel inventory cycles rather than airfare cycles, and hotels typically discount later than airlines do. That pushes the ideal package window slightly later than the ideal flight-only window.

This is our reading of the published sources, not a measurement of our own. We have not run a fare study and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

How far in advance should I book a vacation package?

Four to eight weeks ahead for domestic trips and three to five months for international ones. Those ranges are where the guidance from Google Flights, Expedia and Going overlaps, which makes them the most defensible windows available given that the sources disagree with each other. Because the hotel half of a package follows hotel inventory cycles rather than airfare cycles, and hotels tend to discount later than airlines, package timing sits slightly later than flight-only timing.

Three things everyone believes that are not true

The day you buy barely matters. The day you fly does.

Myth one

Tuesday is the cheapest day to book

Google Flights measured it: shopping on Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday instead of a weekend produced fares about 1.9% cheaper on average. Expedia's 2026 report names Friday as cheapest, about 3% below Sunday.

Both numbers are rounding errors. The myth dates from an era when airlines filed fare sales manually early in the week; continuous automated pricing erased it years ago.

Myth two

Prices drop at the last minute

They generally rise. Cheaper fare buckets sell out as departure approaches and what remains is the expensive inventory. Genuine last-minute deals exist on routes with excess unsold capacity, but they are unpredictable and you cannot plan around them.

This one matters commercially: "last minute vacation deals" and "last minute flight deals" both draw substantial search volume, and most of that traffic is hunting for something that usually is not there.

Myth three

The day you fly is a minor detail

It is the largest lever most travellers ignore. Google data has put Monday to Wednesday departures around 13% cheaper than weekend departures, and Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday appear repeatedly across independent analyses as the cheapest days to fly.

Shifting departure by a day or two routinely saves more than weeks of price monitoring, and on international routes flexibility of one to three days can move the total by hundreds.

What is the cheapest day of the week to fly?

Midweek. Google data has shown Monday through Wednesday departures running roughly 13% cheaper than weekend departures, and Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday recur across independent analyses as the cheapest days to fly. That effect is far larger than any difference in the day you buy the ticket, which measures at around 2 to 3%. If you can move only one variable, move your departure day rather than your shopping day.

The number under the number

What the package price does not include.

A package total looks like a complete number. It usually is not, and the gap between the displayed price and the amount that eventually leaves your account is where a decent bundle becomes an ordinary one.

Checked baggage. Normally an airline charge levied separately, per bag, per direction. On a family of four with two bags each, this is not a detail. Some packages include an allowance and some do not, and the only way to know is to read the fare rules on the booking page.

Resort and destination fees. Charged by the hotel at check-in rather than collected in the package price. Common in Las Vegas, Florida, Hawaii and much of the Caribbean, and frequently a per-night amount that stacks up quickly over a week.

Seat selection. Increasingly unbundled even on full-service carriers. If sitting together matters, price it before comparing.

Transfers. Some resort packages include airport transfers and some quietly do not, which can be a substantial difference at destinations where the airport is an hour from the hotel.

Not all bundles are the same thing

Five things sold under the word “package”.

The word covers products with genuinely different economics. Knowing which one you are looking at tells you whether the comparison on this page even applies.

Type one

Flight plus hotel

The standard bundle and the one this site is built around. Airfare and accommodation, one price, everything else separate. The wholesale-rate mechanism applies fully here, so this is where the calculator earns its keep.

Compare it against the two components and the arithmetic is clean, because there is nothing else bundled in to muddy it.

Type two

Flight, hotel and car

The same mechanism with a third component, and the one where comparisons most often go wrong. Car rental prices swing violently by location and season, so a package that looks good may simply be attaching an expensive car you did not need.

Price the car separately before accepting it as a bonus. At many destinations a rental is the single most volatile line in the trip. Check car rental prices on Expedia before you decide the bundle is doing you a favour.

Type three

All-inclusive resort packages

Flight plus a room where meals and drinks are included in the nightly rate. The comparison is genuinely harder, because the honest version has to price what you would otherwise have spent on food and drink — which depends on how you actually travel.

A family that eats every meal at the resort and a couple who want to eat in town every night get very different value from an identical booking.

Type four

Packages with resort credit

A bundle including a spend allowance at the property — spa, dining, activities. Treat credit as worth less than its face value unless you would definitely have spent that money anyway, because credit is designed to be spent on high-margin items at prices you did not negotiate.

A useful discount rule of thumb: value it at what you would actually have paid elsewhere, not at what the resort says it is worth.

Type five

Cruise and flight packages

A different market with different rules. The cruise fare dominates the total and the flight is often a small add-on sold at a fixed rate, which can be either a bargain or a poor deal depending on your departure city.

Always price the flight separately here. Cruise-line air programmes vary enormously in quality and the convenience premium is sometimes substantial. Cruise listings are on Expedia if you want to compare.

Worth knowing

“Package” can also mean a fare bundle

Airlines increasingly use the word for fare tiers — a bundle of seat selection, baggage and changes, with no hotel involved at all. It is not a vacation package and none of the arithmetic on this page applies to it.

If the word appears without accommodation attached, you are looking at a fare product, not a bundle.

What is included in a flight and hotel package?

Airfare and accommodation for the dates booked, at one combined price. What is usually not included: checked baggage, seat selection, hotel resort or destination fees, parking, airport transfers and local tourist taxes. Some packages add a rental car, resort credit or all-inclusive meals, and those change the comparison substantially. Read the inclusions on the booking page rather than assuming, because they vary between sellers and between individual packages from the same seller.

The part nobody puts on a landing page

What happens when something goes wrong.

This is the genuine non-price argument for bundling, and also its genuine risk. Both deserve stating plainly.

The argument for a package

One booking means one party responsible for the trip. When a flight is cancelled and your hotel night is already paid for, a package seller has a relationship with both halves of your arrangement. Booked separately, the airline owes you something about the flight and the hotel owes you nothing at all, and reconciling that is your problem and your phone calls.

With children, with a group, or on a trip with connections and transfers, that single point of contact has real value that does not appear anywhere in a price comparison.

The argument against

The same consolidation cuts the other way. A package is one contract, so its cancellation and change terms tend to be governed by the stricter of the two components. A refundable hotel rate bundled with a non-refundable fare frequently leaves you with a non-refundable trip.

You also lose the ability to change one half independently. Moving a flight by a day is a routine operation on a standalone booking and can be impossible inside a bundle without repricing the whole thing.

What to check before you pay, every time

The cancellation terms for both halves. Not the headline "free cancellation" badge — the actual terms, which frequently apply to only one component. If the page does not make this explicit, treat the trip as non-refundable.

The change fee and the deadline. Both what it costs and how late you can do it.

Who you call. On a package, the seller. Establish that before departure rather than at an airport at midnight.

Whether the fare is a basic economy product. These are frequently bundled into packages and carry restrictions — no changes, no seat selection, sometimes no carry-on — that are easy to miss inside a combined price.

What your travel insurance and card cover. Coverage terms differ between package bookings and separate ones, and some card benefits are conditional on how the trip was purchased.

Consumer protections for package travel differ substantially between countries and change over time. Nothing here is legal advice. Read the terms on the booking page and check the rules that apply where you are buying.

What happens if my flight is cancelled on a package booking?

You deal with the package seller rather than the airline directly, and that is the main practical advantage of bundling: one party holds a relationship with both halves of your trip, so a cancelled flight and an already-paid hotel night are their problem to reconcile rather than yours. The trade-off is that you cannot act independently — you cannot simply rebook yourself on another carrier and keep the hotel, because the booking is a single contract. Establish the seller's support number before you travel, and read the change and cancellation terms before you pay rather than after.

Beyond the booking

How to save on vacations when the package is not the answer.

Bundling is one lever among several, and it is not the biggest one. These are, roughly in order of how much money they move.

1. Move your dates before you move anything else

Flexibility outperforms every other tactic on this list combined. Shifting a departure from Sunday to Tuesday, or a trip from the first week of a school holiday to the last, changes the total by a percentage no booking trick will match. Price three date pairs before you price anything else.

2. Travel in shoulder season

The weeks either side of peak season carry most of the weather and a fraction of the price, and accommodation discounts more aggressively than airfare does. On a resort trip where the hotel is the larger cost, shoulder season is frequently the single largest saving available.

3. Check a second airport

Secondary airports within an hour's drive routinely price differently on the same dates. Include the parking or transfer cost in the comparison and it still wins often enough to be worth the two minutes.

4. Decide your number before you search

The most expensive habit in travel is refusing to book while hunting for a slightly better price. Fares do not descend tidily; they move in both directions. Set the price you would be happy to pay, and pay it when you see it.

5. Price the package and the components every time

Which is the whole argument of this site. Four minutes, three numbers. Sometimes the bundle wins by a lot and sometimes it loses by a lot, and there is no way to know which without checking.

6. Use points where they are worth using

Airline miles on a long-haul cash fare and hotel points on an expensive night are usually worth more than any package discount. Points also come with the flexibility packages remove. If you hold a meaningful balance, price the points option first and treat the package as the fallback.

7. Watch the fees rather than the headline

Baggage, seats, resort fees and transfers can move a total by a double-digit percentage on a family trip. Compare totals, never headline prices.

8. Book the refundable option when plans are genuinely uncertain

Paying a small premium for a rate you can cancel is not a waste. It is buying an option, and on an uncertain trip that option is frequently worth more than the discount you gave up to get it.

Is it cheaper to book a flight and hotel together?

Often, but not always, and the difference is worth checking rather than assuming. Bundles can undercut separate booking because hotels supply discounted rates to package sellers under contracts that forbid publishing those rates publicly — the rate only becomes visible once it is bundled with a flight. That mechanism is real and it is why packages can beat the price you see on a hotel's own website. It breaks down when you would have used airline points, when the cheapest flight is on a carrier the package seller does not sell, or when local accommodation is cheap and easy to book directly.

How much can you actually save by bundling?

There is no reliable single figure, and any site quoting one precisely is guessing. Savings depend on the route, the season, the hotel's inventory position and how much unsold stock the package seller is holding. The honest method is arithmetic rather than a rule of thumb: price the flight alone, price the hotel alone, add them, and compare against the package total including all fees. The calculator on this page does that comparison for you.

What is a flight and hotel package?

A single booking that combines airfare and accommodation, sold at one combined price rather than as two separate purchases. The seller buys or contracts hotel inventory at a discounted wholesale rate and combines it with a flight, and the traveller pays one total. The practical consequences are that you cannot see the individual price of each component, that changes and cancellations are governed by the package terms rather than the airline's and hotel's terms separately, and that the whole thing is one transaction to manage.

When should I not book a package?

Five situations. When you are redeeming airline miles or hotel points, since packages are cash-only and points usually beat cash. When the cheapest flight is on a budget carrier the package seller does not carry. When you need one half of the trip to be flexible, because package cancellation terms are usually stricter than the components' own terms. When you are staying somewhere with abundant cheap independent accommodation, such as much of Southeast Asia or Central America. And when you are chasing elite status or hotel loyalty nights, since packaged stays frequently do not qualify.

Do hotel loyalty points count on a package booking?

Usually not, and this catches people out repeatedly. Most hotel loyalty programmes exclude stays booked through third-party wholesale or packaged rates from earning points and from counting toward elite night requirements. If you are within reach of a status tier, or you are booking a long stay at a chain you are loyal to, that exclusion can outweigh the package discount. Check the specific programme's terms before booking, because the rules vary by chain.

When is the best time to book a flight and hotel package?

The major published datasets disagree, which is itself the useful answer. Google Flights data puts the domestic sweet spot around 38 days before departure with a cheap zone roughly 28 to 61 days out. Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report identifies 15 to 30 days out as cheapest for domestic economy. Going recommends one to three months for domestic and two to eight months for international. The overlap across all three is roughly four to eight weeks ahead for domestic travel and three to five months for international, which is the range worth planning around.

Is Tuesday really the cheapest day to book?

No. That is the most durable myth in travel, and it has been measured. Google Flights found that shopping on Tuesdays, Wednesdays or Thursdays instead of weekends produced prices only about 1.9% cheaper on average. Expedia's 2026 report actually names Friday as the cheapest booking day, roughly 3% below Sunday, attributing the shift to reduced end-of-week business demand. Either way, the day you buy is a rounding error next to how far ahead you book and which day you fly.

What day is cheapest to fly?

Midweek, consistently. Google's data has put Monday to Wednesday departures around 13% cheaper than weekend departures, and Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday come up repeatedly across independent analyses as the cheapest days to fly. That is a far larger effect than any booking-day difference, and it is the lever most travellers underuse: shifting your departure by a day or two frequently saves more than weeks of price monitoring.

Are last-minute vacation packages a good deal?

Rarely, despite the volume of searches for them. Prices generally rise in the final three weeks before departure rather than falling, because cheaper fare buckets sell out and what remains is the expensive inventory. Genuine last-minute deals exist on routes with excess unsold capacity, but they are unreliable and you cannot plan a trip around them. Expedia's own data does show some short-window savings on international fares, which is a reminder that the picture is genuinely mixed rather than settled.

Can I cancel a flight and hotel package?

It depends entirely on the fare and rate rules bundled inside it, and package terms are usually less forgiving than booking separately. A refundable hotel rate and a non-refundable airfare inside one package generally leave you governed by the stricter of the two. If there is a realistic chance your plans change, either book a package that explicitly states free cancellation on both components, or book separately and accept paying a little more for the flexibility. Read the cancellation terms on the booking page before paying, not after.

Do packages include baggage fees and resort fees?

Not usually, and these are where an apparently good package quietly becomes an average one. Checked baggage is normally an airline extra charged separately, and resort fees are typically collected by the hotel at check-in rather than included in the package price. On a week in Florida, Nevada or the Caribbean a resort fee can add a meaningful amount to the total. Add both to the package price before you compare it against booking separately, which is why the calculator on this page includes fields for them.

Is Expedia cheaper than booking direct?

Sometimes for packages, less often for hotels alone. The package mechanism gives large sellers access to discounted rates that hotels cannot publish, which is a genuine structural advantage on bundles. For a hotel-only booking, direct booking increasingly competes well because chains offer member rates, loyalty points and elite credit that third-party bookings do not earn. The sensible approach is to check both, which takes about two minutes and is the entire point of this site.

What is an opaque or wholesale hotel rate?

A discounted room rate a hotel supplies to a travel seller on the condition that it is not displayed publicly as a standalone price. The hotel protects its published rate — which anchors what everyone else pays — while still filling rooms it would otherwise leave empty. The rate becomes visible to you only when it is bundled with a flight, or hidden inside a total. This is the actual economic reason bundles can beat separate booking, rather than any generosity on the seller's part.

Should I book a package for a domestic weekend trip?

Usually not worth the constraint. On short domestic trips the hotel component is a small share of the total and the flight dominates, so the wholesale rate discount has less to work with. You also lose flexibility and loyalty credit for a modest saving. Packages tend to earn their keep on longer stays, on international trips, and at resort destinations where accommodation is the larger cost.

Four travellers, four different answers

The right choice depends on who is travelling.

The same package, on the same dates, is a good deal for one of these people and a poor one for another. Find yourself.

Usually bundle

The family going somewhere warm for a week

Four people, seven nights, a resort destination, school holidays. This is the archetype the package product was designed around and the case where it most reliably wins.

Accommodation is the dominant cost, so the wholesale discount has a large number to work on. Nobody in the party is chasing elite status. Dates are fixed by the school calendar so flexibility is not being sacrificed — it was never available. And one booking to manage genuinely matters when something goes wrong at an airport with children in tow.

Watch: baggage for four people, and resort fees over seven nights. Both are easy to overlook and both are large at this size.

Usually separate

The frequent flyer with a points balance

Travels often, holds airline miles and hotel points, cares about elite nights. Almost every structural advantage of a package is unavailable to this traveller.

Packages are cash-only, so a points redemption is off the table. Packaged hotel stays generally do not earn points and usually do not count toward elite tiers. And someone who flies frequently is more likely to need a change, which is precisely what bundles handle worst.

Watch: the temptation to bundle anyway for a modest saving. On this profile the loyalty cost is frequently larger than the discount.

Often bundle

The couple taking one big trip a year

No meaningful points balance, no status to protect, one significant holiday and a fixed budget. The genuine toss-up, and the case where the calculator is worth the four minutes.

The deciding factors are usually destination and length. A week in the Caribbean tends to favour bundling. A city break in Europe tends not to, because independent accommodation is plentiful and the cheap fares are often on carriers packages exclude.

Watch: whether the bundled hotel is genuinely the one you wanted. A discount on the wrong hotel is not a saving.

Usually separate

The flexible traveller with an open plan

Dates movable, destination negotiable, willing to change plans for a better price. Flexibility is the most valuable asset in travel, and a package converts it into a fixed contract.

This traveller is better served by moving dates until the separate components are cheap than by accepting a bundle discount on dates they did not choose. Their leverage is the thing packages take away.

Watch: the endless-search trap. Flexibility is only worth something if it eventually converts into a booking.

Are vacation packages worth it?

For some travellers on some trips. They tend to be worth it for families and couples taking a week or more at a resort destination, where accommodation is the larger cost and nobody is sacrificing points or loyalty status. They tend not to be worth it for frequent flyers with points balances, for anyone needing flexibility, for short domestic trips where the flight dominates, and for destinations where independent accommodation is cheap. The only way to know for your trip is to compare the package total against the two components separately, which takes about four minutes.
